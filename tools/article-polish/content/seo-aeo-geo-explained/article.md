## Three Acronyms That Sound Like the Same Thing

You wrote something on the internet. Since then, three different eras have changed what "being found" means:

- **SEO** — the Google era: win the list of blue links.
- **AEO** — the answer era: become the quoted answer when Google or Siri replies directly.
- **GEO** — the AI era: become the source an AI assistant cites when it writes its own answer.

They overlap, they build on each other, and people mix them up constantly. You don't need a marketing course to keep them apart — you need one library.

## The Analogy: One Library, One Librarian

Imagine your website is a **book** in a big public library. The librarian at the reference desk answers every visitor's question. That's all you need.

### SEO — getting your book found

Visitors used to browse the shelves themselves. **SEO is everything that helps them find your book**: a correct catalog card, the right section, a catchy spine title. The win condition: your book appears in the shelf list — ideally near the top — and the visitor walks over and picks it up.

### AEO — getting your book quoted

Then visitors got lazy (in a good way). Instead of browsing, they ask the librarian directly: *"which train do I take?"* The librarian doesn't send them to a shelf — she answers on the spot, reading from whatever books are at hand.

**AEO is writing your book so the librarian can quote it out loud**: each chapter opens with its conclusion, the back cover carries a question-and-answer appendix, and every fact is labeled clearly. The win condition: the librarian quotes *your paragraph, word for word* — instead of summarizing badly from memory.

### GEO — getting your name said

Here's the subtle one. A good librarian says: *"according to a book by Athalla Rizky…"* A careless one quotes you and never mentions your name. **GEO is earning the name-drop** — writing with the credibility markers (sources cited, real numbers, quotes) that make the librarian naturally credit you — and, honestly, *measuring* whether she actually does.

## The Compact Version

| | The question it answers | You win when… | The librarian's move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEO** | Can they find my book? | it's picked from the shelf | points to the shelf |
| **AEO** | Can they quote my book? | your exact words are read out | reads your page aloud |
| **GEO** | Will they name me? | the quote comes with your name | says who wrote it |

Notice the direction: each layer assumes the one before it. There's no point polishing your Q&A appendix (AEO) if the catalog card is wrong (SEO), and no point chasing name-drops (GEO) if your paragraphs aren't quotable (AEO).

## Why This Matters Right Now

More and more questions are answered *at the desk* — Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity. Fewer people walk to the shelves at all. That doesn't kill the library; it moves the game. The sites that get quoted and credited receive attention even when nobody clicks.

And one honest note from practice: **SEO and AEO you can verify this week** — your catalog card is correct, your answers are extractable, tools and validators confirm it. **GEO is slower** — you're watching whether engines start citing you, over weeks. Set a baseline, check again later, and don't panic between checks.

## Three Sentences to Remember

- **SEO makes your book findable.**
- **AEO makes your book quotable.**
- **GEO makes your name travel with the quote.**

If you can hold those three lines, you already understand the difference better than most of the internet.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between SEO, AEO, and GEO?**

SEO optimizes for being found in a list of links, AEO optimizes for having your answer extracted and quoted directly, and GEO optimizes for being cited as the source when AI engines generate their own answers. They build on each other: findable first, quotable second, credited third.

**Which one should I start with?**

Start with SEO basics and AEO together — a correct sitemap and meta tags, plus content shaped as direct answers (conclusion-first paragraphs, an FAQ section, structured data). Both are verifiable within days. GEO follows naturally, but its results take weeks to observe.

**Does GEO work instantly after implementation?**

No. Generative engines re-crawl and re-index on their own schedule, and whether they cite you is partly stochastic. The practical approach is to record a baseline (ask an engine like Perplexity your target questions, note the sources it cites) and re-check every few weeks to watch the drift.
